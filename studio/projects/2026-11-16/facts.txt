Project: Engine Room: The Game of the Century: A chess engine written from scratch reviews Byrne–Fischer, New York 1956 (the Game of the Century) move by move, with the search split across 8 Web Workers. Every part of its thinking is drawn live: the search tree, pruning, hash table, move ordering and threads.
Why it is hard: Everything is real and written by hand. Pieces: a legal chess engine in plain JS. The 0x88 move generator handles castling, en passant and promotion and is checked by perft up to 4,865,609 nodes. The search is PVS with a transposition table, null move, LMR, futility pruning and quiescence, and it has to reach depth 12–14 in 7 s per position. On top of that sits a root-split parallel search over 8 Web Workers that shares no memory. It uses speculative null-window scouts and re-searches, and it must stay exactly correct. The tree is instrumented live from inside the hot loop without killing the node rate. The screen draws a radial tree of about 2,500 nodes plus 11 other dense panels at 60 fps on an integrated GPU while 8 of the 16 threads run at 100%. The pieces are vector paths drawn by hand. No libraries, no images, no glyph fonts.
The science: GAME (D. Byrne – R. Fischer, New York 1956, 0–1, 82 plies): 1.Nf3 Nf6 2.c4 g6 3.Nc3 Bg7 4.d4 O-O 5.Bf4 d5 6.Qb3 dxc4 7.Qxc4 c6 8.e4 Nbd7 9.Rd1 Nb6 10.Qc5 Bg4 11.Bg5 Na4 12.Qa3 Nxc3 13.bxc3 Nxe4 14.Bxe7 Qb6 15.Bc4 Nxc3 16.Bc5 Rfe8+ 17.Kf1 Be6 18.Bxb6 Bxc4+ 19.Kg1 Ne2+ 20.Kf1 Nxd4+ 21.Kg1 Ne2+ 22.Kf1 Nc3+ 23.Kg1 axb6 24.Qb4 Ra4 25.Qxb6 Nxd1 26.h3 Rxa2 27.Kh2 Nxf2 28.Re1 Rxe1 29.Qd8+ Bf8 30.Nxe1 Bd5 31.Nf3 Ne4 32.Qb8 b5 33.h4 h5 34.Ne5 Kg7 35.Kg1 Bc5+ 36.Kf1 Ng3+ 37.Ke1 Bb4+ 38.Kd1 Bb3+ 39.Kc1 Ne2+ 40.Kb1 Nc3+ 41.Kc1 Rc2#. The moves are replayed by matching each SAN move against the engine's own generated legal moves, which is a second correctness check.

ENGINE: one JS source, used by the main thread for SAN, attack maps and SEE, and by the workers through a Blob URL. Board: 0x88 Int8Array(128) with piece lists, side to move, 4 castling bits, en-passant square and halfmove clock. Zobrist hashing uses two 32-bit halves seeded by xorshift32 and is updated incrementally in make and unmake. A move is an int32: from (7 bits), to (7 bits), promotion (3 bits) and flags (capture, en passant, castle, double push). Moves are generated pseudo-legally; a move is legal if the king is not attacked after make. Perft self-test at load:
