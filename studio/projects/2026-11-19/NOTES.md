# Xenon Night: a PWR core, neutron by neutron (Claude Studio 003)

A full 3411 MWt pressurized-water reactor core, simulated live in the browser with no libraries. It is solved as two-group neutron diffusion on a 136 x 136 mesh, with iodine-xenon poisoning, fuel and moderator feedback, and an automatic rod and boron controller. The core runs a night of grid load-follow, then a control rod drops. Six Web Workers run Monte Carlo neutron transport on the same mesh to check the eigenvalue independently. Hand-written WebGL draws the heatmaps; canvas 2D draws everything else.

## What is simulated

- **Core**: the IAEA 2D PWR benchmark (ANL-7416 Supp. 2, Problem 11), mirrored from the published quarter-core map to a full core: 17 x 17 assemblies at a 20 cm pitch, 177 fuel assemblies, 9 rodded ones (Bank D). Mesh 2.5 cm, 136 x 136 = 18,496 cells (15,424 inside the staircase boundary). Published two-group constants, B² = 0.8e-4 cm⁻², boundary -D dφ/dn = 0.4692 φ.
- **Solver** (Web Worker, Float64Array): 5-point mesh-centred finite differences with harmonic-mean face coupling. Plain power iteration (S = νΣf₂φ₂, k ← k ΣS_new/ΣS_old). Each group has a red-black SOR inner (ω 1.60, 3 sweeps per group per outer). Converged when |Δk| < 1e-6 and max|ΔS|/S < 1e-5. The dominance ratio is estimated from successive source differences, and k and the residual are kept for every outer. Every solve is warm-started: macro steps from a flux extrapolated in time over the last two steps, paired measurement solves from their own previous solution.
- **Boot validation**: the clean benchmark (all rods in, no xenon, boron or feedback) gives **k = 1.02943 against the reference 1.02959, Δ = -15.3 pcm**, in 301 outers with dominance ratio 0.977.
- **Operating state**: Bank D blends Σa₂ with f = (228 - steps)/228. Boron adds Σa₂ += 6.8e-6 ppm (measured worth about -7.4 pcm/ppm). Xenon adds σX·X with σX = 2.65e-18 cm². Per-cell feedback: Tm = 565 + 18 P, Tf = Tm + 600 q (q = local relative power × power fraction). Doppler Σa₁ = 0.010 [1 + aD (√Tf - √Tf,ref)], moderator Σa₂ += aM (Tm - Tm,ref). Reference temperatures are the hot-full-power averages (583 K, 1183 K), so the benchmark constants are hot-full-power constants. At boot, aD and aM are calibrated with paired solves: Doppler -2.50 pcm/K (Doppler defect divided by the mean fuel temperature rise) and MTC -15 pcm/K. That gives a measured power defect of about -1810 pcm. Power is normalized as P = H Σ κ Σf₂ φ₂ h² (H 366 cm, κ 3.204e-11 J), which gives the absolute flux used for xenon burnout.
- **I-135 / Xe-135**: integrated per cell with exact exponentials (flux held constant) over each macro step. Macro steps are 5 sim-min in real time and 15 sim-min in the fast-forward. Every 30 sim-min a paired solve with X = 0 gives ρXe = (1/k_noXe - 1/k)·1e5.
- **Control** (quasi-static): power follows the dispatch schedule at 1 %/min (100 → 50 % at 20:00, back at 02:00). Each step: solve, ρ = (k-1)/k, and if |ρ| > 10 pcm, move Bank D by ρ/(differential worth), with the worth taken as a finite difference from the last move, then re-solve. Boron moves at most 2 ppm/min to bring Bank D back to 210 steps. Rotating paired solves measure the boron worth, Doppler, MTC and power defect every 2 sim-hours.
- **Rod drop**: at D3 23:00 the SW diagonal Bank D rod (E13, column 4 / row 12) falls fully in and stays in. Its worth is measured by a paired solve with the rod back at the bank position: about -96 pcm at the drop. Re-measured every 2 sim-hours, it falls to about -13 to -30 pcm as the flux and power leave that corner (both values are shown in the callout and the tilt readouts). When QPTR passes 1.02, a red tech-spec alarm (ACTION: REDUCE POWER) goes to the log and the status chip (ROD DROPPED · QPTR>1.02), and a new alarm line follows every +0.1 of tilt. When QPTR passes 1.2, the log adds 'XE AZIMUTHAL REDISTRIBUTION · DR 0.97x' and 'tilt growth uncontrolled', and the tilt chart tags the trace XENON-DRIVEN · DR · UNCONTROLLED. No operator or protective action is modelled: it is a demo of the uncorrected core, and the log and NOTES say so.
- **Kinetics** for every rod step: the step's reactivity (from the solves before and after the move) goes into the inhour equation (6 Keepin groups, Λ = 2e-5 s), solved for the dominant root by bisection. The panel shows the period, the prompt jump β/(β-ρ), and a backward-Euler 0-120 s response (dt 1 ms). Its precursor bars show each group at t = 120 s of that response: the bar is Cᵢ(120 s)/Cᵢ(0), a tick marks P/P0, and the label is Cᵢ/Cᵢ,eq(P). They change with every rod step: long-lived groups lag (for example 0.83 vs 1.00 after a +21 pcm step). The plant's own precursor inventories are integrated over the real power history, exactly for each linear ramp segment.
- **Monte Carlo** (6 workers, delta tracking): Σt = 1/(3D), buckling leakage counted as absorption, 3D isotropic directions projected on x-y, vacuum outer boundary. It uses a collision estimator, fission banking and systematic resampling to 20,000 neutrons per generation, plus Shannon entropy over the 17 x 17 source. The material snapshot refreshes every 2 sim-hours (even hours); each new run takes 50 inactive generations, then the active mean with σ = std/√n. Until a run has 30 active generations, the screen quotes the previous run, labelled "prev run". The status bar and the panel read the same tally snapshot. The ± value is σ(mean); the per-generation spread (about 950-1000 pcm) is shown next to it. The k and entropy charts are a scrolling window of the last 600 generations across runs: earlier runs are ghosted at 30 % with their final ±3σ band, and amber dividers mark each snapshot. The entropy is continuous because the fission source carries over. One generation is dispatched every second frame (30 per second, 600 k histories/s, about 47 ns per flight, frame-capped). Each worker's panel shows its real compute time against the frame-clock period (busy | wait) and its own histories per second of compute (about 450-900 k/s). The panel shows about 300 recent histories as tracks (fast group violet-white, thermal blue) and a faint shimmer of the live fission source. Speeds are counted against the frame clock.
- **Core labels**: every fuel assembly shows its relative power (3 decimals) and its change versus 1 sim-hour ago in the upper part of its cell. There is no plate, only a dark halo round the digits, so the whole cell shows the heatmap and the tilt and the cold corner round E13 read as colour. Values that round to zero print as a muted 0.000 with no sign. Profile strips show φ1 (blue, with fill) and φ2 (pale cyan) along the E-W and N-S axes, with 0.5 and 1.0 gridlines and the dropped rod's column and row. Their legend sits in a reserved band, so it never covers a trace.
- **Colours**: fast flux is blue and thermal flux teal to cyan in both the field maps and the profiles. Violet is reserved for I-135 and amber for Xe-135.
- **Rendering**: the core map and the four field maps are WebGL2. Each is a 136 x 136 RGBA16F texture with mask-normalized bilinear filtering, a 1D ramp texture and iso-contours. The hero blends between macro steps. Everything else is canvas 2D, with a scheduler that redraws one heavy panel per frame. Measured: about 3 ms of JavaScript per frame (p99 10 ms, worst 20 ms) in the controlled-clock render, and 60 fps through the live real-time phase while all 7 workers compute. The compute plant plots the page's real JavaScript cost per frame (cyan bars) next to the kit's frame interval. The only frames over 30 ms are the first draws at boot and at the fast-forward handoff, all within the first 4.2 s of page time, long before the 35 s warm-up ends.

## Honest caveats and simplifications

- **Reactivity budget**: with the published fuel constants, the core holds only about 36 ppm of critical boron at full power with equilibrium xenon. It cannot ride out the xenon pit, because rods and boron run out and the core goes subcritical. The operating core therefore has νΣf₂ multiplied by 1.04 (a fresher reload, shown on screen as "op. fuel νΣf₂ ×1.040"). The benchmark validation always uses the published 0.135.
- **Xenon worth** comes out at about -3780 pcm, not the -2900 the brief expected. These constants give Σf₂/Σa₂ = 0.65 and a high thermal flux (about 7e13 n/cm²s, since all power comes from thermal fission), so xenon sits near its burnout limit. The pit after the 50 % ramp is correspondingly deep (about -1600 pcm below equilibrium) and peaks about 4 h after the ramp, around midnight, not at 03:00-05:00.
- **Tilt**: the full core is loosely coupled (dominance ratio 0.977-0.985), and only Doppler acts locally (the moderator temperature is core-average by design). The drop gives an immediate QPTR of about 1.08. Xenon then redistributes (it builds in the cold SW and burns out in the hot NE) and the tilt grows to about 1.5 over 4 hours. This is a real xenon-driven azimuthal tilt in this model, larger than a real plant would allow before a runback.
- **QPTR**: with 136 cells the core axes fall on cell boundaries, so the quadrants split exactly (the central assembly row and column count half to each side).
- **Solver cost**: plain power iteration with dominance ratio about 0.98 needs 5-60 outers for a macro step, and about 2000 for the macro step of the rod drop. The outers-per-step sparkline scales to the 98th percentile and draws that spike as a capped red bar labelled with its value. Chebyshev extrapolation was tried and rejected: with ω = 1.6 the group-2 SOR inner has complex eigenvalues and the extrapolation diverges.
- **Fast-forward**: the prewarm runs one 15-minute macro step per frame (239 frames, about 4 s of page time), so it is deterministic in the recording. In a live run it takes 20-35 s of real time, because each step waits for its solve. Real time then runs at 15 sim-min/s and the 35 s warm-up lands near D3 19:30. On camera: the ramp to 50% at video 2 s, the rod drop at about 14 s (red pulse, alarm lines, QPTR past 1.02), the ramp back up at 26 s, and the tilt peaking around 32-36 s.
- **Performance**: an earlier version dropped live frames because of wide additive track strokes and a slower MC kernel. The tracks are redrawn every second frame with cheap joins, and the RNG is inlined in the MC loop. The MC runs one generation every second frame: on a warm laptop, one per frame dipped the live view to 40-50 fps.
- **Monte Carlo σ** is std/√n as specified. In a loosely coupled core, generation-to-generation correlation means the true uncertainty is larger.
