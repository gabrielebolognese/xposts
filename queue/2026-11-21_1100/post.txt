Claude Opus 5.5 ray-traced a spinning black hole photon by photon in 179 minutes for $113.94

What it simulates, how it was built, the real numbers, where it struggled, and how to push Claude Code the same way.

1. A spinning Kerr black hole with the mass and distance of Sgr A star, the one at our galaxy's center. Every pixel is an exact light path, traced backward from the camera on the GPU. Those rays light up a thin accretion disk with a flaring hot spot. A virtual Event Horizon Telescope observes it through the night, and the shadow is checked against Bardeen's analytic curve.

2. Built by Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code, alone, at effort xhigh. No libraries: raw WebGL2, canvas 2D and 7 Web Workers. 179 minutes of work over 262 turns, 4316 lines of code. Build cost $113.94 at API prices. A separate critic reviewed it in 2 rounds and gave a final score of 8.1 out of 10.

3. Shadow diameter: 51.167 µas, off from the analytic curve by 0.000196%. ISCO at 2.0236 M, hot spot period 43.13 minutes, max redshift factor 1.3296. The shader integrates 564224 geodesics per second. Live on a Ryzen 7 5700U with integrated Radeon graphics: 1 to 60 fps, GPU 79.1% average, CPU 100%, RAM 13.64 of 15.3 GB.

4. The hard part was fp32. The radial equation drifted off the null constraint and rays bounced off nothing at r ≈ 50. Fix: re-project the radial velocity away from turning points. Rays near the poles jumped a barrier in one step and drew a column of bad pixels, so steps shrink there. And the brief's 40,960 rays per frame did not fit this GPU. It settled on 8,192 on average.
